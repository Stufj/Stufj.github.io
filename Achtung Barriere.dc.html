Make invisible barriers visible

A virtual city walk and a mixed-reality experience that put you behind someone else's eyes for a few minutes . A blind person, an older person, or someone on the autism spectrum, to make invisible barriers impossible to miss.

The closing film

The question

How do you make a barrier visible to someone who never has to think about it?

My role

Prototyped both the web experience and the MR application. Organized and conducted workshops with stakeholders.

The formats

A city walk in the browser, and a live MR experience for public events.

Why it matters

Achtung Barriere! was a research and journalism project by CORRECTIV.Schweiz and HSLU, part of the larger ZukunftswerkStadt programme, funded by the Gebert Rüf Stiftung.

Its starting point: Swiss city centres belong to everyone, but physical and invisible barriers make them harder to access for some than others. Citizens reported barriers through CORRECTIV's CrowdNewsroom platform.

Our job was to make some of those barriers felt, not just read about, through two connected prototypes.

What came out of it

  • A browser-based city walk with three playable perspectives:
    blind, elderly, neurodivergent
    Each built around one specific invisible barrier.
  • A mixed-reality experience for public events and workshops, with filters you can switch on and off inside a crowd, at a pedestrian crossing, or at a construction site.
  • Citizen reports and testing feedback that fed directly into further reporting with CORRECTIV.

Two prototypes

Web prototype

A city walk built in Unity WebGL, about three minutes per perspective.

Reflection

Three minutes per round turned out to be a lot to ask online. WebGL performance on Apple devices in particular fell short, and even close troubleshooting with our game designer could not fully fix it. The clearest lesson of the project: a good idea still needs to survive the format it is built for.

MR application

The same three scenarios — crowd, crossing, construction site — staged in mixed reality with a filter you can switch on and off.

Reflection

In person, it landed harder than the web version ever could. One participant, who is blind, experienced the entire virtual environment through sound alone. Reducing the controls to just moving around in space was a grate UI choice.

Achtung Barriere! concluded at the end of 2025. The team now continues this line of work in Barrieren-Dialog, again including public-facing formats.
