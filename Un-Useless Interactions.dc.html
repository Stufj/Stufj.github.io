Eight prototypes, one hard lesson, and a workshop format built around a single rule: hand someone an old computer mouse, and take away everything else.

The question

Can a playful, low-tech object make a design convention visible faster than critique can?

The realization

Too many options kill focus. The clearest results only came once everything was reduced to one object, a secondhand mouse.

The output

8 prototypes, 5 workshops, and a shareable format now live at
un-useless.art.

Why it matters

Interfaces increasingly look and work the same: rectangular screens, smooth surfaces, a tasteful minimalism now reproducible by AI in seconds. What drops out is the body, since design happens virtually, though interaction is always physical. Restricting myself to found, low-tech material put the body back in: absurd, funny, working objects turn out to be a faster way to make a convention visible than any critique of it.

From research to a repeatable format

  • A research through design thesis: eight speculative prototypes, from an induction stove to an Arduino arm, later finetuned through Chindōgu, bricolage and tinkering.
  • One realisation: real focus needed a single technical restriction, an old computer mouse, taken apart and repurposed.
  • That became the rule for a standalone workshop, refined over four runs at HSLU's MediaDock, HEK Basel, E-Werk and a makerspace, and shown as a poster at World Usability Day 2025
  • The finished format is free to run, at un-useless.art
  • One of the prototypes, Toilet Doomscrolling, later became a full scale piece for a gallery show
