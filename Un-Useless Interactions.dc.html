Non-nonsense
prototyping

Eight tangible prototypes built from one second-hand computer mouse — to show that physical interaction design needs no sensors, no code and no budget.

See the prototypes · un-useless.art

Scroll Sporen — a cart wheel on the scroll wheel of an opened mouse, tied to a shoe. Lower your heel while walking and the feed advances.

The question
Can tangible interaction be built with the simplest technical means?
The constraint
One computer mouse. Three inputs. Zero lines of code.
The output
8 prototypes, 1 workshop, 69 pages — submitted Sept 2025

Why it matters

Interfaces increasingly look and feel the same — rectangular screens, smooth surfaces, the same tasteful minimalism, now reproducible by AI in seconds. What drops out is the body: design happens virtually, though interaction is always physical.

Restricting myself to a two-franc mouse put the body back in. Absurd, funny, working objects turn out to be a faster way to make a convention visible than any critique of it.

What came out of it

A repeatable method — Chindōgu, bricolage and tinkering as one working process
A workshop run with no code, where every participant left with a built object
A third column for Dunne & Raby's A/B manifesto: C — Subversive

The theory behind it
Chindōgu

Kawakami's usefully useless objects: they must work, and they must be unusable in public. Ten rules keep the humour honest.

Bricolage

Build with what is already lying around. The material at hand decides the form — buying a part is a defeat.

Tinkering

Understanding through fiddling rather than planning. The prototype is the argument, not its illustration.

“Today you can get far in a design process without ever having touched or physically made anything yourself.”Elke Rentemeister, interview 2025
